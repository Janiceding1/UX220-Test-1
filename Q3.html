Tim Berners Lee and the World Wide Web

Original Research

Tim Berners Lee worked at CERN, a physics research centre, where scientists had difficulty sharing research and documents across different computer systems. In 1989, he proposed the World Wide Web to make sharing and accessing information easier.

Current Uses of the World Wide Web

Today, the World Wide Web is used for communication, education, entertainment, shopping, banking, and research. Unlike its original purpose of sharing scientific information, the web now connects billions of people around the world for personal and professional activities.

Comparison

Both the original World Wide Web and today's web focus on sharing and accessing information. However, the original web was designed mainly to help researchers share documents, while today's web supports many different activities for people around the world.
